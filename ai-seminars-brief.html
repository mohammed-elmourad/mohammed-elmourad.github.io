For feedback · Not a pitch
AI · Education · Sydney · 2026

An idea for teaching high school students how to actually use AI.

A sponsor-funded program. Free to schools. Built around what students will actually find useful over the next two years — not generic "AI literacy". This is a working document, not a finished plan. Read it, then tell me what's wrong with it.

01 The idea

Teach students the AI workflows they're already trying to figure out on their own.

Students are using AI tools — clumsily, secretly, sometimes in ways that hurt their learning. Most schools are reactive: they ban it, restrict it, or quietly hope teachers will sort it out. What's missing is a serious, student-facing program that treats AI as a study skill the way past generations treated note-taking, essay structure, or research.

I want to build that program and deliver it in person at high schools across Sydney. Run by someone close to the audience in age, not a corporate trainer or a consultant who hasn't sat an exam in twenty years.

How I actually use this stuff for my own uni work — not "intro to AI tools".
02 How it pays

Sponsors pay. Schools get it free. Students get the program.

The school-pays model doesn't work. School PD budgets are tight, the established paid competitors charge under $800 for multi-session AI programs, and big tech (Amazon, Google, AWS + Code for Schools) is rolling out free national AI literacy at scale through 2026. Competing with free, for student attention, with school budgets, doesn't add up.

Sponsor-funded does. A brand whose offering is genuinely useful to students — a university running future-student recruitment, a bank teaching financial literacy, an EdTech platform with student-facing tools — pays for the program. The school hosts at zero cost. The sponsor gets credible reach into a hard-to-target demographic. Students get the content free. I get paid by the sponsor, not the school.

03 Already ruled out

Three obvious alternatives that don't survive contact with the numbers.

Deprecated
Selling seminars direct to schools

Tiny budgets. The established paid competitor (IAMAI) charges $795 for a four-session program. Free national programs are funded by Amazon, Google, UNSW. School-as-buyer makes the math impossible.

Deprecated
Teacher PD as a side hustle

Different buyer, content, sales motion. As a uni student selling to teachers, the credibility battle is constant. Hours come straight out of sponsor-building time. The runway hedge is already covered by existing tutoring income.

Deprecated
Licensing content to schools to distribute (B2B2C)

Even harder. Established EdTech players charge $20–50 per student per year and take 6–18 months to close through state-level procurement. An unknown solo operator against free national alternatives doesn't crack that.

04 Already true

Not everything is theoretical.

I've spoken to a principal directly to test whether private schools allow sponsor-branded programs. The answer was nuanced — there's a real conflict-of-interest concern that needs handling, but the model is viable provided the sponsor's offering is genuinely useful to students and the COI is transparent. The principal confirmed sponsor-funded programs have been delivered at private schools before.

I have direct relationships with principals at both general and Islamic private schools in Sydney. That access is the unfair advantage — not the seminar content itself, which any competent operator could build. The distribution is the moat.

The first 6–12 months are about running 2–3 free pilot seminars to generate the case study assets a sponsor actually buys: real student-facing engagement data, testimonials, content reach. Once those exist, the sponsor pitch has something to point at.

05 Still open

The honest gaps.

  1. The actual sponsor shortlist. Categories yes (universities, banks, EdTech, professional services, tech recruiting future talent). Five specific named brands with a contact, an angle, and a reason they'd say yes — not yet.
  2. The COI pitch. When a principal asks "how do you handle the conflict of interest?" — needs a rehearsed answer. Three angles: sponsor-content separation, sponsor-student alignment, transparency and opt-out. Not yet scripted.
  3. Pricing structure. Flat fee per program, per school, per student reached, or hybrid. Want to benchmark against Start Smart / Digital Thumbprint delivery partner rates first.
  4. The deliverable spec. What is the seminar, exactly? One hour, three sessions, which year levels? Determines costs, capacity, sponsor cheque size.
  5. Pre-pitch validation. Before sinking time into a polished deck — one or two informal brand contacts to test whether the proposition would even get a meeting.
